# Approved scripts

Scripts Lana has approved and is ready to record. One `## ` section each,
newest last. This is the end of the pipeline: an idea gets pitched in a brief,
kept in the reader, written out when it is worth writing, and lands here once
she has signed off on the words.

A script here is final. Nothing edits it without being asked — it is what she
reads on camera, and a silent improvement to a sentence she has already
approved is the one change that is never wanted.

Entries carry a `**From**` line when the script came from a briefed idea, so
the sources and tags can be found again. A script written outside a brief has
no `**From**` line, which is fine.

## Renting living brain cells

**Script**
You can now rent living human brain cells to run your code.

A company called Cortical Labs is offering access to biological computers through a platform called Cortical Cloud, with plans starting at around $300 a week.

These systems use actual human neurons grown from stem cells and placed onto silicon hardware. Developers can interact with them through software, including a Python API.

So instead of renting compute from a traditional cloud provider, you're effectively renting access to a living neural network that can adapt and learn from feedback.

And that matters because modern AI has a scaling problem. The larger our models become, the more electricity and compute they require, and that cost keeps increasing.

Biological neurons are incredibly energy-efficient compared with conventional computer hardware, which is why researchers are interested in whether biological computing could eventually complement, or even replace, parts of today's AI infrastructure.

We've spent decades trying to make computers behave more like brains. Now, we're starting to experiment with using actual brain cells as the computer.
