# Basis Point — Friends

Channels worth learning from, and the specific device to take from each. Not a
list of people to imitate: the point is to name the *mechanic* — the thing the
video does to the viewer — and decide whether it transfers.

Each channel carries one or more videos studied frame by frame: the on-screen
hook, what the video physically is, and the beat structure with timestamps. The
spoken lines are read off the burned-in captions, so they are the script as the
viewer receives it. Where a line was too small to read cleanly it is marked.

Entries are newest first. A `## ` heading with a **Reach** field is a channel; a
`### ` heading inside one is a studied video. Run
`node scripts/brief-to-json.mjs --friends` after editing.

## Devices worth stealing

The cross-channel findings, ranked by how well they transfer to what Lana
already makes.

- **Ask a question only the video can answer, then make answering it public.**
  covacut opens on hand-lettered text reading "I have a question for you" then
  "Which one is the real me?" — 11M views, and the comments are people guessing.
  askcatgpt's first caption line is "what did you see two videos ago" and the
  comments are people answering from memory. jasoncooperson writes "I'll let you
  be the judge of how well it did." Three channels, three subjects, one device:
  the video sets a test and the comment box is the answer sheet. It composes
  directly with the artifact format — show the Claude-made thing and ask which
  part a human did.
- **Put the credential and the promise in a frame that never leaves.**
  nicktarmo letterboxes the whole video: a black surround with "Part 1", a
  subtitle reading "20 year old finance student explains how to get rich in the
  next 10 years", and the footage playing in a window in the middle. Anyone who
  arrives at second forty still gets the premise. Lana's videos currently make
  the premise once, in the first sentence, and then it is gone.
- **Give permission to skim.** In that same frame: *"press here for 2x speed."*
  It converts the impulse to leave into a tap, and a tap is a signal.
- **Explainers get shared; news gets liked.** askcatgpt: 12.2K shares on 11.9M
  views, about 2%. The GPU reel: 187 on 292K, about 0.06%. Thirty times the
  share rate. A video someone sends to one specific person is a different
  distribution engine from one they argue under, and Bucket C is where it lives.
- **Name the villain in the first third.** askcatgpt's caption three seconds in:
  "Force 1 = tech companies. They get paid more $ the longer you stay here, so
  they wanna hook u. Bad." Unhedged, and it gives the viewer somebody to be
  angry at who is not the creator.
- **Write the caption to the person who stopped watching.** askcatgpt's opens
  "R u reading this caption because the video started to get boring? Ur gonna
  check the comments next huh?" Lana's captions currently restate facts the
  script already delivered.
- **Number the parts.** "Part 1" (nicktarmo), "ep. 6" (covacut). Serialisation
  makes a hit pull people backwards through the catalogue, and the miss shows up
  as demand — askcatgpt's promised follow-up produced "I can't find the second
  video 😢" with 6 likes.
- **A comment-keyword CTA manufactures comments, not arguments.**
  jasoncooperson's "Comment EDIT for the FULL tutorial" produced 936 comments
  against 661 likes — more comments than likes, and almost every one is the
  single word "Edit". Worth knowing precisely because it looks like enormous
  engagement and is really a mailing list. Use it when there is something to
  sell, and never mistake it for the thing the GPU reel did.

## @covacut

**Profile** Editing, AI tools and creative-tech demos, in a numbered episodic format.
**Reach** 24 recent reels: median roughly 200-250K, with outliers at 11M, 4.7M, 4.4M and 3.6M.
**The model** Do a visually obvious thing with AI, to herself, and make the
video a puzzle before it is a demo. The tools only appear once the viewer has
already committed to guessing.
**Steal this**
- The guess-which-one-is-real cold open. It converts a demo into a quiz and a quiz into a comment.
- Hand-lettered text as the entire visual language — no template, no lower thirds, just marker-style words over the shot.
- Episode numbers, so a hit sends people backwards through the catalogue.
- A question the audience answers for free: "Does this now mean legally your online identity/image is now the legal property of…?" is a whole explainer handed over in a comment.
**Don't copy** The tool-tagging. That reel credits and hashtags HeyGen,
ElevenLabs and ChatGPT — exactly the free promotion Lana has decided is
sponsorship inventory.
**Link** https://www.instagram.com/covacut/

### ep. 6 how to clone yourself — 11M views

**Numbers** 504.2K likes · 3.4K comments · 541 shares · 59 seconds · posted March 26, 2025
**On-screen hook** Teal hand-lettered text over a warm, low-lit bedroom shot: "I have a **Question** for you." at 1s, then "Which one is the **Real Me?**" at 3s in large hand-drawn caps.
**Caption** "ep. 6 how to clone yourself 👯 / did u get it right??"
**What it actually is** Vlog-grade footage — sitting on the floor of a bedroom, warm practical lighting, no studio — with every graphic hand-drawn in marker style over the top. At 5s the frame splits into multiple versions of her with "Is it me?" scrawled beside one. The back half is screen recording: the HeyGen interface at 21s, a script document at 30s, and the generated result at 42s.
**Beats**
- 0:00-0:04 — the question, stated as text, before any explanation
- 0:04-0:12 — the versions of her on screen at once; the viewer is asked to pick
- 0:12-0:35 — the reveal and the build, in screen recordings of the tools
- 0:35-0:59 — the result, and the caption pushes the guess back into the comments
**Steal** The order. The test comes first and the method second — the demo is the payoff for having guessed, not the subject. Lana's tool demos currently open on what was built.

## @askcatgpt

**Profile** Explainers about attention, technology and the brain, drawn as stick figures on a whiteboard.
**Reach** 24 recent reels: median roughly 150K, with outliers at 11.9M, 3.5M, 3.2M, 1.4M and 1.2M.
**The model** Take something the viewer is doing *right now*, prove they are
doing it, explain the mechanism, and name who profits.
**Steal this**
- The meta-caption that narrates the viewer's own behaviour as it happens.
- The two-force explanation: one villain with an incentive, one piece of biology. No notation, no jargon, and both halves are arguable.
- A memory challenge the viewer answers from their own experience — infinitely repeatable and free to produce.
- The share mechanic. 12.2K shares; an explainer people send to a specific person in their life outperforms one they merely agree with.
**Don't copy** The "like and follow and I will explainnnn" close and the withheld
part two. It works for him and it reads as bait, which is the opposite of the
register Lana's scripts are written in.
**Link** https://www.instagram.com/askcatgpt/

### The real reason you can't stop scrolling — 11.9M views

**Numbers** 509.5K likes · 5.7K comments · 12.2K shares · 97 seconds · posted August 12, 2025
**On-screen hook** A serif title card burned into the top of the frame — "The real reason you can't stop scrolling" — that stays there for the first fifteen seconds and returns later.
**Spoken open** Read from the burned-in captions: "what did you see two videos ago" (0:00) · "don't click back and look" (0:02) · "but can you actually remember" (0:04) · "and if not then you are right now" (0:06) · "in the middle of something — unaware use" (0:08) · "and it is 100% not your fault" (0:10).
**What it actually is** One continuous talking-head take in a public workspace — glass, ceiling ducts, a real whiteboard behind him on castors — with a lav mic clipped on and a marker in hand. No cutaways, no b-roll, no stock. The whiteboard is the graphic: pre-drawn stick figures at the start, and over the video two numbered sections fill in, with "Tech Companies" written in red at around 45s. He turns and draws on it live from about 15s.
**Beats**
- 0:00-0:12 — the memory test, then the absolution: it is not your fault
- 0:12-0:20 — the term is named on screen, "unaware use"
- 0:20-0:40 — force one, the villain: "social media companies make money by keeping you here"
- 0:40-1:10 — force two, the biology: "you like the dopamine hit when you swipe," drawn on the whiteboard as section 2
- 1:10-1:37 — what to do, and the promise of the next video
**Steal** The whiteboard. It is the cheapest possible motion graphic — it gives the video a second visual channel, it forces the argument to have a structure the viewer can see, and it costs nothing but a pen. For a channel that explains mechanisms, this is more transferable than any editing technique on this page.

**Reach** 24 recent reels: median roughly 40K, with a pinned 1.3M and a sponsored 810K.
**The model** Personal-brand advice with a high production floor, monetised
through brand deals rather than reach.
**Steal this**

- The "press here for 2x speed" line — permission to skim, printed on the video.

**Don't copy** The engagement profile. The 1.3M reel's comments are "Bro is
helpful af" and "🔥🔥" — big views, inert audience. That is the motivational
register, and it does not produce the comment sections Lana's two hits ran on.

### the biggest gold rush is right now (Part 1) — 1.3M views

**Numbers** 103 seconds · pinned · comment section almost entirely emoji and praise
**On-screen hook** Not a sentence in the footage — a *frame*. Black surround, "Part 1" centred at the top, "press here for 2x speed" in small text beside it, and under that a two-line premise: "20 year old finance student explains how to get rich in the next 10 years." All of it present at 0.8s and still present at 77s.
**Spoken open** From the captions below the video window: "the biggest gold rush" (0:00) · "25 years is right now" (0:02) · "I'm a 20…" (0:05).
**What it actually is** A letterboxed layout, not a full-bleed reel: the footage plays in a window in the middle of the frame with the title block above it and the caption line below, so roughly a third of the screen is furniture that never changes. The footage itself is him writing on a wall-mounted whiteboard, plus a large text card of the promise.
**Beats**
- 0:00-0:05 — the claim, said fast, over the standing frame
- 0:05-0:20 — the credential: who he is and why he is saying this
- 0:20-1:20 — the three things to learn, written up on the wall
- 1:20-1:43 — the close, and the pointer to part two
**Steal** The frame, and only the frame. Someone landing at second forty knows what they are watching, who is talking and that there is a part two — three facts Lana's format currently delivers once and then drops.

## @jasoncooperson

**Profile** 186K followers, 119 posts. "Helping business owners master and implement AI." DM "WORK" for done-for-you solutions; runs a Skool community, Leverage Lab. Credited in Lana's own Claude-edit caption.
**Reach** Modest against the follower count — visible reel tiles run 22.5K to 53.2K. This is a funnel, not a reach play.
**The model** Content qualifies buyers. The pinned reel is the same artifact
format Lana ran, ending in a comment-keyword CTA that turns viewers into a list.
**Steal this**
- "I'll let you be the judge of how well it did." One sentence that licenses the comment section to render a verdict — the same device covacut and askcatgpt use, on exactly Lana's subject.
- The arguable thesis stated flatly in the caption: "You don't need keyframes or adjustment layers anymore. The technical side of editing is getting replaced by AI. All that's left is the creative side."
- The DM/comment keyword, *if and when there is something to sell*.
**Don't copy** The keyword CTA as an engagement tactic. It produced 936 comments
on 661 likes — more comments than likes — and they are almost all the single
word "Edit". That is a mailing list wearing a comment section, and it would
hollow out the thing that makes Lana's comment sections worth having.
**Link** https://www.instagram.com/jasoncooperson/

### Everything you just watched was edited by Claude — pinned

**Numbers** 661 likes · 936 comments · 11 shares · 54 seconds · posted July 24
**On-screen hook** Heavy display type slammed over the talking head at 0.6s: "WHAT YOU'RE WATCHING".
**Caption** "Comment "EDIT" for the FULL tutorial 👇 / Everything you just watched was edited by Claude inside Premiere Pro. / It opened Premiere, cut all the footage, generated the graphics, and edited the whole video itself. I'll let you be the judge of how well it did."
**What it actually is** Talking head in an RGB-lit desk setup, cut against full-screen motion-graphic sequences on a flat blue background — editing icons (scissors, film reel, refresh arrows) dealt across the screen like cards at 2-4s, and full-frame text cards such as "YOU'RE THE …" at 19s. The graphics are the proof: they are what Claude made, and they are most of the runtime.
**Beats**
- 0:00-0:05 — the claim, over the face, in the biggest type on screen
- 0:05-0:18 — the motion-graphics sequence, which is simultaneously the demo
- 0:18-0:40 — what it means for editors: the technical side is going, the creative side is not
- 0:40-0:54 — the CTA, comment the keyword
**Steal** The claim's placement — before any explanation, in type large enough to read at a glance, over a face — and the invitation to judge. Both are things Lana's 101k reel did in the caption but not on the screen.
